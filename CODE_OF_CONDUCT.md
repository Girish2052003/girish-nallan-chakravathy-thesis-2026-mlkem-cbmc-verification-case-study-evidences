# Code of Conduct

## ML-KEM CBMC Verification Case-Study Evidence

This repository supports open scientific scrutiny, technical discussion,
reproducibility and responsible collaboration.

Participants are expected to engage with each other professionally and to
focus discussion on evidence, methodology, implementation behaviour and
research claims.

## 1. Expected conduct

Participants should:

- communicate respectfully;
- criticise ideas, claims, code and evidence rather than individuals;
- distinguish demonstrated facts from interpretation or speculation;
- provide evidence for technical claims where reasonably possible;
- acknowledge mistakes and corrections;
- preserve relevant attribution and provenance;
- respect legitimate disagreement;
- avoid intentionally misleading representations of another participant's
  work;
- avoid knowingly submitting fabricated or manipulated research evidence; and
- handle security-sensitive information responsibly.

Strong technical disagreement is acceptable.

Personal attacks are not.

## 2. Unacceptable conduct

Unacceptable behaviour includes:

- harassment;
- threats;
- discriminatory or degrading comments;
- deliberate intimidation;
- sustained personal abuse;
- publication of another person's private information without a legitimate
  basis or permission;
- knowingly fabricated evidence;
- intentional misrepresentation of authorship or provenance;
- knowingly false accusations of misconduct;
- malicious disclosure of credentials or private security information; and
- deliberate disruption that prevents reasonable scientific discussion.

## 3. Scientific criticism

Criticism of the repository, thesis, methodology, verification strategy,
assumptions, evidence or conclusions is welcome.

Disagreement with a research conclusion is not misconduct.

Replication that produces a conflicting result is not misconduct.

Identification of an error is not misconduct.

The preferred response to scientific disagreement is evidence, analysis,
reproduction and clear argument.

## 4. Research integrity

Participants should not knowingly:

- alter evidence while presenting it as the historical original;
- remove attribution in order to imply different ownership;
- manufacture experimental results;
- conceal a material correction;
- misrepresent generated verification checks as independent theorems or
  experiments;
- claim verification outside the assumptions, bounds or domain actually
  established; or
- misrepresent third-party work as original repository-author work.

Where an error is discovered, an auditable correction is preferred over
concealment or silent rewriting.

## 5. Security-sensitive material

Security-sensitive reports should follow `SECURITY.md`.

Do not publish credentials, private keys, access tokens or unnecessarily
detailed unpublished vulnerability information in public discussions.

## 6. Scope

This code applies to participation in repository-controlled collaboration
spaces associated with this project, including issues, pull requests and
repository discussions where available.

It governs participant conduct.

It does not determine:

- copyright ownership;
- software or documentation licensing;
- academic authorship;
- scholarly citation;
- validity of scientific conclusions; or
- ownership of third-party material.

Those matters are governed by their applicable evidence, policies, licences
and academic standards.

## 7. Moderation

Repository maintainers may edit, hide, close, lock or remove contributions
that materially violate this code or interfere with responsible operation of
the repository.

Serious or repeated misconduct may result in restriction from repository
participation where the platform provides such controls.

Moderation decisions should not be used to suppress legitimate technical
criticism merely because it challenges the research.

## 8. Good-faith corrections

Participants are encouraged to correct their own mistakes.

A good-faith technical error should normally be addressed through correction
and clarification rather than personal accusation.

Claims of fabrication, misconduct or deliberate deception should not be made
casually and should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Participants should also consult:

- `SECURITY.md`;
- `LICENSE.md`;
- `REUSE_AND_LICENSING.md`;

## 10. Core principle

The purpose of this repository is to enable evidence-based scientific
scrutiny.

Participants may disagree strongly while still preserving accuracy,
traceability, attribution and respect.
